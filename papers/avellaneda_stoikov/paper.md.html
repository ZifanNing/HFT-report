# Avellaneda & Stoikov (2008) — High-frequency trading in a limit order book

Quantitative Finance 8(3), 217–224.

## 1. 问题与设定

A single risk-averse dealer quotes a bid and an ask around a mid price that follows an
arithmetic Brownian motion `dS = sigma dW` (no drift). Market orders arrive as Poisson
processes whose intensity decays exponentially with the distance of the quote from the
mid: `lambda(delta) = A * exp(-k * delta)`. The dealer maximises expected exponential
utility of terminal wealth `-exp(-gamma * (X_T + q_T * S_T))` at a fixed horizon `T`,
with `X` the cash and `q` the (unbounded) inventory. The paper is a simulation paper: there
is no empirical dataset, prices are dimensionless (`s0 = 100`, `T = 1`).

Frequency: continuous time, discretised at `dt = 0.005` per step (200 steps per session).

## 2. 模型 / 策略公式

Indifference (reservation) price for inventory `q` at time `t`:

```
r(s, q, t) = s - q * gamma * sigma^2 * (T - t)
```

Optimal total spread (bid–ask distance around `r`, first-order approximation):

```
delta_a + delta_b = gamma * sigma^2 * (T - t) + (2 / gamma) * ln(1 + gamma / k)
```

Quotes: `p_bid = r - delta/2`, `p_ask = r + delta/2`. As `t -> T` the inventory term
vanishes and the spread collapses to `(2/gamma) ln(1 + gamma/k) ~= 2/k`.

## 3. 原文参数

`s0 = 100`, `T = 1`, `sigma = 2`, `dt = 0.005`, `q0 = 0`, `gamma = 0.1` (also 0.01, 0.5),
`k = 1.5`, `A = 140`, 1000 Monte Carlo paths; orders of size 1; the mid moves by
`+-sigma sqrt(dt)` each step; fills are Poisson draws with intensity `lambda(delta) dt`.

## 4. 原文声称的结果

Table 1 of the paper (gamma = 0.1, from memory of the published table — verify against the
PDF before quoting): the inventory strategy earns a mean profit of ~65.0 with std ~6.6 and
final-inventory std ~2.9, against ~68.4 / ~12.7 / ~8.7 for the symmetric strategy that quotes
the same spread centred on the mid. The main claim is qualitative: centring quotes on the
reservation price instead of the mid gives slightly lower mean PnL but a much lower PnL
variance and much tighter inventory distribution, for every gamma tested. The spread narrows
as `t -> T`, and larger gamma gives a wider spread and smaller inventory.

## 5. 复现所需的平台能力

- Layer: **Strategy** (`hft/strategy/base.py::BaseStrategy`), no Model / Signal layer.
- Fidelity: L2 + queue model. The paper's fill model is a Poisson intensity in distance;
  here fills come from the replayed order flow through `Naive` and `ProbQueue`. The two
  queue models bracket the answer.
- Event loop with timers (`requote_ms`), asynchronous submit/cancel with entry/response
  latency, inventory tracking (`ctx.position`), maker/taker attribution, markout by time
  and by mid change, and `hft compare` for the comparison matrix.
- Online volatility estimate from the replayed mid (rolling realised variance).
- Units on this platform: prices in ticks, time in seconds, inventory in clips of
  `order_size`. `k` is therefore per tick and `sigma` in ticks / sqrt(s); the paper's
  dimensionless `gamma` does not transfer, only the product `gamma * sigma^2 * (T - t)`
  (ticks of skew per clip) is meaningful.

## 6. 复现前的预期

Written before any run.

1. **The core claim (lower inventory variance at similar PnL) should hold** on
   `synthetic_dev`: the reservation-price skew is a pure inventory-mean-reversion device and
   does not depend on the fill model. Expect `risk.qty_abs_mean` and `qty_max` clearly
   smaller for AS than for `SymmetricQuoter`, with PnL of the same order.
2. **Absolute PnL will not match the paper and will be near zero or negative.** The paper's
   fills are exogenous Poisson draws that earn the full quoted distance. On a replayed
   random-walk book, a resting quote at or behind the touch is filled mostly when the touch
   is swept through it, i.e. exactly when the price moves against us. Expect maker markout
   at 1–5 s to be negative (adverse selection) and the spread earned to be ~1 tick, so total
   PnL for both strategies ~ 0 minus adverse selection. Fees are zero on `synthetic_dev`.
3. **ProbQueue fills fewer orders than Naive.** Naive fills our whole order on any trade at
   our level; ProbQueue requires the queue ahead to be consumed first. Expect fewer fills,
   lower turnover, and the Naive PnL to be the optimistic end of the interval. Not certain:
   Naive also gets more adverse fills, so the ordering of PnL could flip if adverse
   selection dominates spread capture.
4. **PnL degrades with latency.** With entry latency, quotes are placed on a stale book:
   orders that cross on arrival become taker fills and stale quotes get picked off. Expect
   monotone degradation 0 -> 1 ms -> 10 ms and a non-zero taker share at 10 ms. On
   `synthetic_dev` events are ~10 ms apart, so 1 ms should barely matter and 10 ms should.
5. **The finite-horizon spread will mostly be behind the touch.** With `k = 1.5` per tick
   the spread term is ~1.3 ticks; adding the inventory term makes the model spread wider
   than the 1-tick market spread for most of the session, so AS posts 1+ ticks behind the
   touch and is filled less often than the baseline that joins the touch. As `t -> T` it
   tightens. Expect AS fill count < SymmetricQuoter fill count.
6. **On the Binance sample nothing is a conclusion.** (Written against the 7-minute smoke
   recording; the final runs use `binance_btcusdt_20260918`, 78 min with 4 holes, same
   instrument and fees.) Minutes of data, and the
   instrument carries 10 bps maker and taker fees, while one tick on BTC at ~80k is
   ~0.001 bps. PnL after fees will be negative and equal to roughly minus fees for both
   strategies; only PnL before fees, fill counts and markouts are worth reading. Expect
   ProbQueue fills to be far fewer than Naive because BTC top-of-book queues are large
   relative to a 0.001 BTC order.
7. Not testable here: the paper's variance-of-PnL claim across 1000 paths (we have one
   path per dataset); `A` (arrival intensity) plays no role since fills come from data.
