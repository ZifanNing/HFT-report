# Results — Avellaneda & Stoikov (2008)

Synthetic runs: `synthetic_dev` (SYN, 200k events, 1374 s, tick 0.01, zero fees), params from
`configs/base.yaml` (gamma 2e-4, k 1.5, T = session, requote 100 ms, order 1 lot, max
inventory 10 clips, join touch). Real runs: `binance_btcusdt_20260918` (78 min incl. 705 s of
holes, 10 bps maker and taker fees, 0.001 BTC clips, max inventory 5 clips, gamma 5e-10,
`configs/btc.yaml`). Baseline `SymmetricQuoter` with `half_spread_ticks = 1` (joins both sides
of a 1-tick book). Latency cells set `entry_us = resp_us`. Side-by-side HTML:
`runs/compare_as_{syn,btc,sens}.html`. The parameter sensitivity was re-run out of sample in
Wave B and now lives under `sweeps/` — see the sensitivity section. All runs on the final
matching semantics (cross-fill only
when the opposite best moves through, crossing remainder as taker, whole-lot ProbQueue fills).

**Caveat on the real data.** 78 minutes of one bursty BTCUSDT session with fees 1000× the
tick is a pipeline check, not a conclusion. Read "PnL before fees" (= `pnl.total +
costs.fees`) for the strategy effect; after fees every BTC cell is minus its fee bill.

## Sensitivity (AS, zero latency, synthetic_dev; OUT OF SAMPLE)

**This replaces the Phase 1 in-sample table.** That table chose and reported the same
parameters on the same 1374 s session, so it could not separate a real effect from a lucky
cell. Here the grid is scored on train windows only and every one of the 36 cells is re-run
on the held-out test window of the same fold; every number below is a **test-window** number.

The two parameter tables in this file answer different questions and must not be read the
same way:

| table | kind | in-sample risk |
|---|---|---|
| "Fixed table" above | **comparison** - queue model and latency are venue / fidelity facts, nothing is chosen | none; no selection happens |
| this section | **selection** - gamma, k, touch_mode, horizon are knobs we would pick | the whole point; the diagnostics below say how much the ranking is worth |

Setup. `configs/sensitivity.yaml` (Naive) and `configs/sensitivity_pq.yaml` (ProbQueue) on

(3x3x2) zipped with `(rolling_horizon, horizon_s)` in {(false, T), (true, 30 s)} = 36 cells.

The whole-grid Spearman is the number docs/05 asks for, but on this grid it flatters the

place behind the touch). The multiple-testing threshold assumes independent cells and is
therefore conservative, and several "different" rows above are the same experiment.

### What the out-of-sample re-run actually says

1. **Under Naive the ordering is real; the winner is not.** Spearman 0.785 over the grid, the
   same five cells top both splits, and the best cell clears the multiple-testing floor by 7x.
   But the train winner is not the test winner in any of the three sweeps, the modal winner
   holds only 2 of 3 folds, and one cell's fold-to-fold noise (271 /day) is over half the base
   cell's entire test PnL (456 /day). The table supports "join the touch and keep
   gamma*sigma^2*tau around a tick per clip" - a region, not a cell. Anything finer is noise.
2. **Under ProbQueue the sensitivity table is not evidence of anything.** 34 of 36 cells lose
   money out of sample, the two that do not fill 0.5 times a minute, and the train ranking of
   the top five is anti-correlated with test (-0.91). The Phase 1 claim that particular gamma
   and k values are better is a Naive-queue-only claim, and Naive is the optimistic bound.
3. **On BTC the whole table is noise around the fee bill.** The spread across 36 cells is
   +/-10% of a -4300 /day fee bill, the best train cell does **not** clear its own noise
   threshold (-4343 against -2760), and the top-18 Spearman is 0.16. 53 minutes of one session
   cannot separate these parameters. This is the headline negative result and it is the
   honest answer: on this data the sensitivity question is unanswerable, not answered.
4. **Three Phase 1 sensitivity claims do not survive.**
   - "`behind` mode loses money (-9.61)": out of sample it is +10.3 /day (+0.16 per session),
     indistinguishable from zero. It is clearly *worse* than `join` (rank 19 vs 7), but the
     loss itself was in-sample noise.
   - "a rolling 30 s horizon drives PnL to ~0 (-0.29)": out of sample it is 463.5 /day, equal
     to the base cell (455.9), and it is the largest rank move in the grid (17 on train -> 6
     on test). See the horizon caveat below before reading it either way.
   - "k = 5 earns more than k = 1.5 (13.83 vs 10.42)": the direction survives (489.8 vs 455.9)
     but the gap is 34 /day against 271 /day of fold noise, so it is no longer a finding.
   Two claims do survive: gamma 5e-5 beats the base cell (1207 vs 456, rank 3 on both splits),
   and k = 0.5 barely fills (15.7 fills/min vs 188.6).
5. **Horizon caveat.** `horizon_s` stays at the base 1400 s while the sweep windows are 275 s
   and 153 s, so tau runs 1400 -> 1125 s and never decays to zero. The non-rolling arm here is
   a "constant tau ~ 1250 s" arm, not the finite-horizon decay a full-session run shows. This
   axis measures the *level* of the skew term, not its decay, which is why the rolling-vs-fixed
   comparison differs from the Phase 1 one. Re-testing the decay itself needs a dataset long
   enough to hold several full horizons; `synthetic_dev` is not one.
6. **`requote_on_touch_change` was not re-run out of sample.** It is an order-management knob
   rather than a model parameter, and adding it as a fifth axis would double the grid to 72
   cells and raise the multiple-testing threshold for every other row. Its Phase 1 row (+9.85
   vs +10.42, 30407 orders vs 16450) remains in-sample only and must not be quoted as an
   out-of-sample result.

## What held, what did not (against paper.md section 6)

1. **Core claim held, and more strongly than in the paper.** On SYN, AS keeps mean
   |inventory| at 0.5–0.6 clips against 5.4–5.8 for the symmetric quoter, which spends most
   of the session pinned at the 10-clip cap (holding periods 11 s vs 1.4 s). The paper trades
   a little mean PnL for that; here AS also has the *higher* PnL in every SYN cell (+10 vs
   −19 under Naive, −12 vs −66 under ProbQueue) because the un-skewed quoter accumulates a
   directional position on a random walk and pays for it in drawdown (22–72 vs 0.4–13). On
   BTC the inventory effect is there too (2.0 vs 3.4 clips under Naive) but PnL before fees
   is a wash (−44 vs −38): both lose ~$30–45 on ~$1M traded, and everything else is fees.
2. **Absolute PnL ~0, as predicted; sign depends on the queue model.** Under Naive the SYN
   1 s markout vs fill price is +0.17 ticks (a third of the half spread captured), under
   ProbQueue −0.27: with a real queue we are filled only once the queue ahead is gone, i.e.
   when the level is about to be swept. Naive is the optimistic end of the interval,
   ProbQueue the pessimistic one, and the interval contains zero. Adverse selection vs mid is
   small on SYN (−0.01 to −0.04 ticks) — the generator has no momentum — and huge on BTC
   (−130 to −800 ticks at 1 s): every fill on this bursty session is a sweep.
3. **ProbQueue fills fewer orders: held everywhere.** SYN 3685 vs 6839 (AS), 8708 vs 15340
   (Sym); BTC 6095 vs 13737 and 6339 vs 16881. Whole-lot ProbQueue fills (post-change) cut
   AS fills further than before but the PnL ordering Naive > ProbQueue held in all SYN cells.
4. **Latency degrades PnL monotonically: held on SYN, reversed on BTC.** SYN AS 10.42 →
   10.33 → 8.71, Sym −19.2 → −20.5 → −28.7, taker share 0.5% at 1 ms and 3–4% at 10 ms as
   predicted. On BTC 1 ms of latency *improves* PnL before fees (−44 → −32 AS naive, −36 →
   −27 PQ) and the maker share drops to 0.83 / 0.70: on a book that moves 100s of ticks per
   100 ms, a quote that arrives stale often arrives *marketable* and is filled as taker at
   the current best, which is better than the price we quoted (markout vs fill price goes
   from −271 to −131 ticks). With maker fee = taker fee (10 bps) there is no penalty for that.
   This is a real effect of the price-improvement rule, not a bug, but it only helps because
   the fee schedule is symmetric and the session was one-directional in bursts.
5. **AS quotes mostly behind the touch: held. `behind` mode "loses money": did not hold.**
   Mean model spread 2.38 ticks vs a 1-tick market, so the bid usually sits 1 tick behind and
   AS fills less than half as often as the touch-joining baseline; you must be at the touch to
   be filled by anything other than a sweep, and `behind` is clearly the worse setting
   (out-of-sample rank 19 of 36 vs 7 for `join`). The in-sample −9.6 PnL did **not** survive:
   out of sample `behind` is +10.3 /day, i.e. zero. See the out-of-sample section.
6. **Finite horizon matters for inventory; the PnL half of the claim did not survive.** With
   T = session end the skew term decays to zero and the spread tightens from ~3.4 to 1.33
   ticks. A rolling 30 s horizon makes the skew per clip 45× smaller and inventory balloons to
   ~4 clips (out of sample: 3.79 vs 0.26 for the base cell) — the inventory control is entirely
   the `gamma sigma^2 (T−t)` product, which must be ~1 tick per clip to act at all. But the
   in-sample "PnL goes to ~0" did **not** hold: out of sample the rolling cell earns 463.5 /day
   against the base cell's 455.9, and it is the largest rank move in the grid (17 on train → 6
   on test). The out-of-sample windows are shorter than the horizon, so that section tests the
   *level* of the skew term rather than its decay; the decay claim is untested, not refuted.
7. Surprises: (a) k is not monotone as the paper suggests: k = 5 (spread floor 0.4 ticks)
   earns more than k = 1.5 because it joins the touch more often, k = 0.5 (floor 4 ticks)
   almost never fills — the intensity model behind k is not what fills orders here. Out of
   sample only the second half of that survives: k = 0.5 still barely fills (15.7 fills/min vs
   188.6), but k = 5 beats k = 1.5 by 34 /day against 271 /day of fold-to-fold noise, so the
   k = 5 > k = 1.5 ordering is no longer a finding.
   (b) Requoting on every touch change doubles the order count for no PnL gain (9.85 vs
   10.42) but lowers inventory further — **in-sample only, not re-run out of sample**. (c) BTC ProbQueue costs 4× less in fees than Naive
   and ends with the same PnL before fees; on a fee-heavy venue the pessimistic queue model
   is the one that matters.

## Robustness of the fixed table (paired block bootstrap, 2026-09-22)

The fixed table and the `sweeps/sweep_d15a2ae7` half-split above report each ordering claim as a
point number. This asks a different question: **how much of each difference is within-session
noise?** Rather than re-simulating, it resamples the realised PnL path. The two arms of a
comparison are paired on a common 1 s grid, so the market both of them saw cancels in the
difference; what is left is resampled with a circular block bootstrap at block lengths of 5, 15,
30 and 60 s, 2000 replicates. The widest block is the most conservative and is the one judged.
Intervals spanning a data hole are dropped. Reproduce with

```
hft robust papers/avellaneda_stoikov/configs/robust_syn.yaml
hft robust papers/avellaneda_stoikov/configs/robust_btc.yaml
```

Both use PnL **before fees**; see the BTC note below for why that is not optional.

"Naive beats ProbQueue" is the strongest of the three: it clears zero by a wide margin in both

table reports is inside the noise of this session. The claim survives for the Symmetric quoter and
partially under ProbQueue, so it is not wrong in general; it is not established for the strategy
the paper is about, on this data.

These are PnL **before fees**. Measured on net PnL the reversal is far larger and entirely
uninformative: fees are 96% of the loss on this file (AS/naive nets −1153.79 with a fee bill of
1109.87), so on net PnL the arm that trades less always wins — ProbQueue fills 6,095 times against
Naive's 13,737 and "beats" it by 18,038 /day while losing on PnL before fees. That number is a fee

five of seven are significantly the other way.

takes 13,737 fills against 13,298 at 10 ms; Naive takes 13,737 against ProbQueue's 6,095), and BTC
AS markout at 1 s is −182 ticks under Naive and −680 under ProbQueue, so the marginal fills a
faster or more optimistic arm wins on this session are adverse. That story fits the sign, but this
analysis does not establish it: PnL here includes mark-to-market on inventory, and two arms holding
different inventory through a trending session can produce the same sign with no difference in fill
quality. Testing it needs a fill-level decomposition, which is not done.

### What this interval does and does not cover

The bootstrap resamples **within one session**. It answers "how much of this difference is noise
inside this session" and nothing else. It cannot estimate session-to-session variation, because
there is one session: a tight interval here is compatible with the sign flipping on another day.
Every BTC number above should be read as "on 2026-09-18", and the SYN numbers as "on this
generator with this seed". That limit is not a property of the method; it is the sample.

## Known limitations

- One path per dataset: the paper's variance-of-PnL claim across 1000 paths is untested.
- gamma is chosen per dataset so that `gamma sigma^2 T` is ~1–2 ticks per clip; the paper's
  dimensionless gamma = 0.1 has no direct meaning on a tick grid.
- The sensitivity table is now out-of-sample (walk-forward, 3 folds), but the folds are
  short: 275 s train / 153 s test on SYN, 645 s / 400 s on BTC. One cell's fold-to-fold
  noise is 271 /day (SYN Naive) to 913 /day (BTC); that is the resolution of these tables.
- The 36-cell grid has only 23 distinct outcomes: the tick grid maps different (gamma, k)
  pairs onto the same quotes, so the effective grid is smaller than the nominal one.
- `horizon_s` is not rescaled to the sweep window, so the non-rolling arm of the
  sensitivity grid tests the level of gamma*sigma^2*tau, not the finite-horizon decay.
- `requote_on_touch_change` was not re-run out of sample; its Phase 1 row is in-sample only.
- BTC: 4 holes (705 s) during which nothing is quoted; inventory carried across a hole is
  marked at the post-resync mid, so hole jumps enter PnL as inventory marks.

- The robustness intervals are within-session only. With one recorded session there is no way to
  estimate how much a difference would move on another day, so a tight interval is not evidence of
  generalisation. See the robustness section.
- The circular block bootstrap wraps the series end to start, and on BTC the series is five
  gap-free windows concatenated, so a few blocks pair increments that are minutes apart in reality
  (at block 60 s, about 4 of 67 blocks straddle a window boundary). This widens nothing and is
  small, but the intervals are not exactly those of a single contiguous series.
- The BTC reversal of "latency degrades PnL" is reported as a measurement, not explained. The
  adverse-selection reading is consistent with the recorded markout but is not demonstrated;
  inventory mark-to-market is not separated from fill quality.
