every BTC statistic in this file.

### Which windows these numbers use, and what a hole does to them

Checked in Wave B, because a published R² that silently straddles 707 s of missing data is not
a result. `evaluate.py` loads the whole span in one frame; it does not restrict itself to
gap-free windows. `binance_btcusdt_20260918` is **5 gap-free windows** (298.6 / 113.8 / 8.6 /
337.9 / **3226.0** s, 84.9% coverage) separated by **4 holes** totalling 707.6 s, the largest
568.6 s. `synthetic_dev` is a single gap-free window of 1374.1 s, so nothing below applies to it.

**Contemporaneous regression (the paper's claim): not affected.** `bucket_ofi` already drops
every bucket whose predecessor bucket is missing (`gap == 1` filter), so a hole longer than one
bucket removes itself. Re-running the whole evaluation restricted to W4 alone — 3226 s, no
holes, 81% of covered time — confirms it (`runs/signals/cont_ofi_btc_w4/`, reproduce with
`python -m papers.cont_ofi.evaluate --dataset binance_btcusdt_20260918 --symbols BTCUSDT
--start 1789743695914000000 --end 1789746921914000001 --out runs/signals/cont_ofi_btc_w4`):

| window | R² full span (published) | R² W4 only | beta full span | beta W4 | beta x 2D W4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 0.505 (n=3984) | 0.515 (n=3226) | 45–53 | 45.97 | 350 |
| 5 s | 0.676 (n=796) | 0.641 (n=645) | | 49.65 | 378 |
| 10 s | 0.677 (n=399) | 0.695 (n=322) | | 52.91 | 403 |
| 30 s | 0.690 (n=133) | 0.719 (n=107) | | 49.66 | 378 |

Differences are ±0.035, smaller than the window-to-window variation, and every conclusion in
the "What held" section is unchanged. **The published R² numbers stand.**

**Forward-looking signal table: mildly affected, biased the wrong way.** The labels in
`hft.eval.labels` do cross holes. Event horizons (`shift(-h)`) jump the gap outright; time
horizons use as-of semantics, so they mostly *under*-measure into a hole rather than jumping it,
except where the hole is shorter than the horizon. Counting the affected rows on the BTC file:

| horizon | rows whose label crosses a hole | mean abs mid change, within-window | ... crossing a hole |
|---|---|---|---|
| e1 | 8 (0.02%) | 44.8 ticks | 4,433 ticks |
| e10 | 44 (0.12%) | 410 | 7,931 |
| e50 | 204 (0.57%) | 1,432 | 7,887 |
| t1s | 37 (0.10%) | — | 310 |
| t5s | 192 (0.54%) | — | 1,060 |
| t30s | 948 (2.67%) | — | 3,167 |

At e10 those 44 rows are 0.12% of the sample but carry ~31% of the total squared variation of
the label. That inflates the denominator of a linear R² without being explainable by OFI, so the
published forward R² is biased **down**: on the hole-free W4 window `ofi_e10` gives 0.027 /
0.055 / 0.055 at e1 / e10 / 1 s against the published 0.022 / 0.040 / 0.050, and `imbalance`
gives 0.056 / 0.110 / 0.106 against 0.047 / 0.086 / 0.106. Spearman IC is rank-based and barely
moves (`ofi_e10` 0.270 / 0.330 / 0.325 on W4 vs 0.274 / 0.327 / 0.314 published; `imbalance`
0.365 vs 0.371 at e1). **Conclusion: the forward R² column understates by roughly 15–30%
relative, the IC column is unaffected, and no ordering or conclusion changes.** The tables
below are left at the full-span values, with this note as the correction.

Request to the Quant Analyst: `hft.eval.labels.make_labels` has no notion of session windows,
so any future signal evaluation on a dataset with holes inherits the same bias. A
`windows=` argument that nulls labels crossing a gap would fix it at the source.

## What held, what did not (against paper.md section 6)

1. Contemporaneous linear impact: **held on both datasets.** beta > 0, R² grows with the
   window as the paper reports (0.50 -> 0.69 on BTC, 0.86 -> 0.91 on SYN; the BTC 10 s value
   of 0.68 is right at the paper's 65% average). The synthetic R²
   is above the paper's 65% average; predicted 20–50%, so the generator is "cleaner" than a
   real book (every sweep is an OFI event, size refills are pure noise that averages out at
   1 s). Surprise: on SYN `beta * 2D = 0.77–0.86`, close to the stylised-model value of 1
   with no tuning; on BTC it is ~390, i.e. the observed impact per unit of top-of-book flow is
   two orders of magnitude larger than best-level depth would predict. That is what a
   book sparse in ticks does: the paper's `D` is depth per tick, and at $80k with a $0.01
   tick the next level is often 100s of ticks away, so one unit of flow moves the mid by
   100s of ticks. Not a contradiction of the paper, but the stylised `1/(2D)` needs depth per
   tick, which `top_of_book` does not carry (feature request: depth within N ticks).
2. OFI as a forward predictor on SYN: **~0 as predicted** (|IC| <= 0.02, R² < 0.001). The
   flow signal carries no information on a memoryless generator. Not predicted: imbalance /
   microprice have a small but real IC (0.056, t ~ 12) because the generator's partial
   trades deplete a thin best level and can delete it, so queue size does predict the next
   mid move. `synthetic_dev` is a null control for flow signals, not for state signals.
3. OFI as a forward predictor on BTC: **positive but not what I predicted.** IC 0.2–0.3 at
   1–10 rows (predicted 0.02–0.08) and decaying with horizon as predicted. The size comes from
   same-sign runs of batch-level mid moves during sweeps (the momentum baseline has IC 0.33
   at 1 row and a 97.6% hit rate), so it is 100 ms batching plus a bursty session, not a
   tradable edge at the touch. The predicted ordering imbalance ~ microprice > OFI holds
   (0.37 vs 0.27 at e1, 0.45 vs 0.33 at e5); last-mid-change > OFI at e1 did not.
4. Last-mid-change on SYN: IC ~0 (predicted slightly negative from bid–ask bounce; there is
   no bounce in a mid series so ~0 is the right answer, my prediction was wrong).
5. `beta ~ 1/(2D)` across instruments: not testable with one instrument per dataset.

## Data characteristics of the BTC file (checked before interpreting)

I first read the BTC numbers as a data bug; a lag test says otherwise, so this is recorded to
save the next reader the detour.

- On the pre-reconversion file the reconstructed book was never crossed and the spread was
  1 tick in 99% of rows, yet only 42% of trades printed inside the touch (median 4 ticks off,
  90th pct 808 ticks), and 1,206 single 100 ms batches moved the mid by > 500 ticks ($5),
  max 18,653 ticks. The reconverted file (holes marked, 14/14 replay checks) gives almost
  the same signal statistics, so these are properties of the session, not of the converter.
- 1 s changes of the book mid and of the last trade price have correlation 0.95 at lag 0 and
  < 0.2 at +-1 s; shifting trade timestamps by -5 s .. +5 s only lowers the within-touch
  rate. So the depth and trade streams are the same market with consistent clocks.
- Conclusion: the session was fast and bursty. sigma of the mid is ~850 ticks/sqrt(s), i.e.
  ~1 bp/sqrt(s), which is ordinary BTC volatility at a $0.01 tick; 91% of 100 ms batches do
  not move the mid and the rest move it in same-sign runs (sliced market orders sweeping a
  book that is sparse in ticks at $80k). Trades sit "off the touch" because the touch is the
  last 100 ms batch, not the state at trade time.
- Consequences for the numbers above: (i) `beta * 2D ~ 380` instead of ~1 is expected when
  price levels are ~100s of ticks apart: the paper's `D` is depth *per tick*, ours is depth at
  the (sparse) best level; (ii) the large forward IC and 97.6% hit rate of the momentum
  baseline come from same-sign runs of batch-level mid moves during sweeps, which is a real
  feature of 100 ms batched data but not exploitable at the touch (the move has happened
  inside the batch), so it says nothing about tradable predictability.
- **Confirmed 2026-09-22** by `python -m hft.data.cli audit binance_btcusdt_20260918`: it is
  staleness, not a converter fault. Measured against the touch of the *last* depth batch the rate
  is 42.8%; allowing the touch of the *next* batch as well it is **99.2%**. Trade-to-batch
  staleness has median 48 ms and p99 99 ms — half a batch and one batch, which is what uniform
  arrival inside a 100 ms window predicts. The same audit replays the stored parquet and
  reproduces the converter's own 14 snapshots compared / 0 mismatches / 9 resets, and finds 0
  crossed books and 0 rows inside a hole.

## Known limitations

- One instrument per dataset, so the paper's cross-instrument claim `beta ≈ 1/(2D)` is not
  testable here. The single-instrument value is off by two orders of magnitude on BTC for a known
  reason (below), which is not the same as confirming or refuting the paper.
- `beta·2D ≈ 390` on BTC because `top_of_book` carries depth at the best level, while the paper's
  `D` is depth *per tick*. At $80k with a $0.01 tick the next level is often hundreds of ticks
  away. Testing the stylised constant needs depth within N ticks — a feature that does not exist
  yet (request to the Data Engineer, recorded above).
- The forward-looking signal test is a platform standard, not a paper claim. The large BTC IC
  (0.2–0.3) comes from same-sign runs of mid moves *inside* 100 ms batches; the move has already
  happened when the batch prints, so it is not tradable at the touch and must not be quoted as
  predictive power.
- `synthetic_dev` is a null control for flow signals only. Its imbalance/microprice IC is real
  (0.056, t ≈ 12) because partial trades deplete a thin best level, so it is not a null control
  for state signals.
- The 42% within-touch trade rate is explained by 100 ms depth batching: 99.2% of trades fall
  inside the bracket of the touch before and after, with median staleness 48 ms (`hft.data.cli
  audit`, 2026-09-22). This is measured inside our own data; it is still not cross-checked against
  an unbatched stream, since `@bookTicker` was not recorded. The consequence for signals stands
  either way — the touch a trade is measured against is up to one batch old.
- BTC numbers come from one 78-minute session with 705 s of holes. Labels crossing a hole are
  dropped, so the effective sample is smaller than the row count suggests.
